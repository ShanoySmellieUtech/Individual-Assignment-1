7 Cures for a Lean Purse

After reading "7 Cures for a Lean Purse," I learned how to build wealth and maintain it. The chapter taught several things that I can apply to my life.

My biggest takeaway was the first cure: Start thy purse to fattening. In essence, it means saving one tenth of all my earnings; it may look small at first, but it will eventually add up. As the Jamaican proverb says, “Every Mickle mek a muckle”, it reminds me that small savings can make a difference as they accumulate over time.

The other cures taught me valuable financial lessons. I learnt to control my spending, invest in a safe space, protect my savings, the importance of owning a home, preparing for the future, and improving my ability to become wiser and earn. I believe that these lessons can help me in the near future to make better financial decisions.

5 Laws of Gold

After reading the 5 Laws of Gold, I acquired knowledge on saving and investing wisely.The first two laws are quite similar to some of the 7 cures of a lean purse.

They speak on how gold comes to those who put away one tenth of their earnings and those who invest wisely. The third law explains that gold stays with those who invest based on the advice of the experienced and wise. The fourth law expounds on gold being taken away from those who invest in businesses that they don't know or businesses not approved by experienced people.

Lastly, my biggest takeaway was that gold leaves those who follow the advice of tricksters and schemers. This taught me to not be hasty and be careful of scammers. Conclusively, these laws have taught me about investments and being careful about where I invest and with whom I invest my money.

As a university student I understand that it is of utmost importance that I learn these lessons on financial responsibility and making wise financial decisions in this stage of my life. By reading these chapters it will not only help me now but in the future when I eventually move in the working world.
